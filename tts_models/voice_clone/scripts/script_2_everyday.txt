# SCRIPT 2 of 3 — Everyday life, opinions, description
# Runs ~11 minutes at the pace measured on script 1 (150 wpm of speech).
# Same room, same mic, same distance as script 1.
#
# HOW TO RECORD
#   - TURN VOICE ISOLATION OFF. Control Centre > Mic Mode > Standard.
#     Measured twice on your own voice: it costs 23.2pp of speaker identity
#     (98.7% -> 75.5% on identical passages), because it strips sub-300Hz chest
#     resonance. Worse for training, it punches hard digital-zero holes INSIDE
#     sentences -- 44 interior gaps, median 223ms, 19.7% of clip duration --
#     erasing the breaths and plosive stops the model must learn pause structure
#     from. It does not hurt transcription at all, which is why it looks safe.
#   - Voice Memos, one continuous file, ~1s between passages.
#   - Fumbled a line? Pause two seconds and redo that passage from its
#     start. The aligner keeps the better take and drops the other.
# Read it like you are talking to a friend, not reading aloud. Slight
# imperfection is fine and actually helps — the model learns your natural
# rhythm, not a newsreader's.
#
# This script deliberately covers different ground from script 1: shorter
# sentences, more questions, numbers, place names, and a wider emotional range.

1. It rained all morning and then cleared up around two, which is exactly the opposite of what the forecast said.

2. I have stopped checking the weather app. It is wrong often enough that I may as well look out of the window.

3. Coffee first, then anything else. That is not a preference, that is a requirement.

4. I make it the same way every day. Same amount, same water, same mug, and I would notice immediately if any of that changed.

5. There is a small place near the office that does a good filter coffee for about forty rupees.

6. It takes maybe seven minutes to walk there, which is long enough to think about something and short enough not to feel like a trip.

7. I ordered the same thing for about three months before I tried anything else on the menu.

8. That is a habit I have in restaurants too. Once something works, I stop exploring.

9. My friend finds this unbearable. He orders something different every single time, and about half of it is a mistake.

10. He would say that half is the point. I am not convinced, but I admire the commitment.

11. We went out for dinner on Saturday and the place was completely full, so we ended up somewhere neither of us had heard of.

12. It was better than the original plan, which is annoying because it means he was right.

13. The food came out too fast, though. I never trust it when the food arrives in four minutes.

14. Cooking at home is mostly about planning. If the shopping is right, the cooking is easy.

15. If the shopping is wrong, you spend an hour improvising and it never quite works.

16. I have been trying to cook more this year. Not for any dramatic reason, just because I like knowing what is in it.

17. Rice, dal, something green, and whatever vegetable looked good. That covers most weeknights.

18. Sunday is different. Sunday I will spend two hours on something that takes twenty minutes to eat.

19. Is that a good use of a Sunday? Probably not. Do I enjoy it? Enormously.

20. Sleep is the thing I get wrong most consistently. I know exactly what I should do and I do not do it.

21. No screen for an hour before bed. Same time every night. Everyone knows the list.

22. Knowing the list and following the list are apparently unrelated skills.

23. I sleep better when I have exercised, which you would think would be motivating.

24. I walk about five kilometres most days, usually in the evening, usually while listening to something.

25. Podcasts, mostly. Occasionally music, but I find I stop paying attention to music and just keep walking.

26. There is a route near my place that loops past a park and a school and back around. It takes about fifty minutes.

27. In December it is dark by six, so the whole walk happens under streetlights, and it feels completely different.

28. Winter here is not really winter. It is just a few weeks where you need a jacket in the morning.

29. I grew up somewhere that got properly cold, and I do not miss it at all.

30. Although there is something about cold weather that makes a hot drink genuinely satisfying rather than just pleasant.

31. Travel is the thing I keep meaning to do more of and keep not doing.

32. I have a list of about eight places. I have been to two of them.

33. The problem is that a good trip needs about ten days, and ten days is hard to find.

34. Weekend trips are fine but you spend most of them in transit.

35. I went to Goa in March for four days and spent roughly eleven hours travelling. That ratio is not great.

36. It was still worth it. The sea does something to your head that a photograph of the sea does not.

37. I took about two hundred photos and looked at maybe six of them afterwards.

38. That is the strange thing about photos. Taking them feels important and looking at them almost never happens.

39. My phone tells me I have thirty-one thousand pictures. I have no idea what is in most of them.

40. Every few months I decide to sort them out. I have never once finished.

41. Books are the same. I buy them faster than I read them and I have made peace with it.

42. There are about fourteen on the shelf I have not started. I look at them and feel slightly guilty and then buy another one.

43. I finished one last week that I had been reading for four months, mostly ten pages at a time.

44. It was good, but I wonder how much of it I would have absorbed if I had read it in a week instead.

45. Reading on a phone is not the same. I get about three paragraphs in before something interrupts.

46. Notifications are the enemy of anything that requires sustained attention.

47. I turned almost all of them off about a year ago and I have never regretted it.

48. Nobody has ever needed me urgently in a way that a notification would have solved.

49. Music is background for me most of the time, which I feel slightly bad about.

50. Occasionally I will sit and actually listen to an album from start to finish, and it is a completely different experience.

51. My taste has barely changed in ten years. I am aware that is a slightly embarrassing thing to admit.

52. Films I am worse at. I will start one, watch forty minutes, and then not go back for three weeks.

53. Series are easier because the episodes are already the right size.

54. Although I have abandoned more series than I have finished, usually somewhere in the second season.

55. Sport I follow loosely. I know the results, I rarely watch the whole thing.

56. Cricket is the exception. A good test match is five days of something that could go any way, and nothing else is like that.

57. Nobody who did not grow up with it understands why five days is a feature rather than a problem.

58. I tried explaining it to a colleague from Berlin and it did not go well.

59. He asked what happens if it rains and I had to explain that sometimes nothing happens for a day and that is fine.

60. Language is the thing that surprises me most when I travel. Not the difficulty, the assumptions.

61. You do not realise how much you rely on tone until you are somewhere the tone means something else.

62. I speak three languages badly and one properly, and the badly ones are more useful than I expected.

63. People are extremely forgiving about grammar if you are clearly trying.

64. Money is boring to talk about and important to think about, which is an unfortunate combination.

65. I set up an automatic transfer on the first of every month and I try not to look at it.

66. That has worked better than every budgeting system I have attempted.

67. The trick was removing the decision, not improving the discipline.

68. That is probably true of most habits. Make the good option automatic and the bad option slightly inconvenient.

69. My phone stays in another room while I work now. Not because I am disciplined, but because walking is annoying.

70. Family is the thing that stays constant while everything else changes, and I do not call home often enough.

71. Every conversation starts with an apology about how long it has been, which is a strange way to begin.

72. My mother asks the same four questions in the same order and I find it oddly comforting.

73. Are you eating properly, are you sleeping, how is work, and when are you coming home.

74. The answers are usually yes, not really, fine, and I am not sure yet.

75. I moved cities four years ago and I still describe the other one as home about half the time.

76. I do not know when that switches over, or if it does.

77. New places take about a year before the streets stop needing a map.

78. There is a specific moment where you take a shortcut without thinking about it, and that is when it becomes yours.

79. I remember exactly where I was the first time that happened here. Outside a chemist, going towards the station.

80. Friendship gets harder to maintain as everyone's schedules stop overlapping.

81. The people I see most are not the people I am closest to. That took me a while to notice.

82. Someone told me that adult friendship is mostly logistics and I think that is depressingly accurate.

83. So I try to be the one who suggests the date, because otherwise nothing gets arranged.

84. Six months goes past very quickly when nobody sends the message.

85. Anyway. That is most of what I would say if you asked me what I have been up to.
